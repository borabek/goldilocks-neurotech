# Idea 2 (NeuroBridge): Alzheimer's disease, animal and human testing of hydrogels

Compiled 2026-11-07 from 5 web searches. Companion to `idea2-hydrogel-animal-human-testing.md` (Parkinson's, ADHD, ADHD-like diseases). Alzheimer's is not one of the three diseases listed for the project, so treat this as an optional fourth case or a comparison set.

**How far to trust it.** Abstracts, PMC excerpts and repository pages only (status A); no full texts opened. Many rat studies use chemically induced amnesia models (scopolamine, trimethyltin, streptozotocin, aluminium chloride) that mimic memory loss, not Alzheimer's pathology. Comparators differ (oral tablet, oral suspension, marketed product), so do not pool numbers.

## 1. Bottom line

- **Human testing of a hydrogel for Alzheimer's: none found.** The one clinical-trial hit was a patent for aluminium oxyhydroxide (Alhydrogel, a vaccine adjuvant suspension), which is not a hydrogel therapy in the sense used here.
- **Animal work is plentiful and mostly intranasal.** Dozens of rat and rabbit papers put cholinesterase inhibitors or natural compounds into thermosensitive or ion-triggered nasal gels. Few use a transgenic amyloid model.
- **Two routes beyond the nose:** subcutaneous or intramuscular depot gels (sustained release of donepezil or curcumin), and intracerebroventricular gels that change the brain environment.

## 2. Intranasal in situ gels

| Gel and cargo | Model | Result | Caveat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Chitosan/beta-glycerophosphate thermosensitive gel, D-penicillamine (Front Aging Neurosci 2021, PMC8081912) | APP/PS1 transgenic mice, 2 mg/kg intranasal; gel vs liquid vs vehicle | Improved cognition, less amyloid-beta production via ADAM10 and melatonin receptor 1 | Mouse numbers per group small (n=16 total per experiment) |
| Thiolated chitosan hydrogel with liposomal donepezil (Sci Rep 2019, PMC6606601) | Rabbits | Mean brain drug content 107% higher than oral tablets | Pharmacokinetics only |
| Donepezil nanostructured lipid carrier in situ gel (RSC Pharm 2024;1:820) | Rats, trimethyltin neurodegeneration | Cmax 193.41 ng/mL at 2 h; release 89.51%; particle 112.5 nm; pharmacodynamic benefit reported | Chemical model |
| Donepezil gels, chitosan or Pluronic F-127 with Transcutol P (Gels 2022;8:715) | RPMI 2650 nasal cell line, ex vivo | Good tolerance in cells; Pluronic + Transcutol P raised permeation | No animal data in the excerpt |
| Donepezil + resveratrol in situ gels (Nirma Univ) | Rats, scopolamine amnesia | Cognitive gain vs marketed donepezil and resveratrol suspension | Chemical model |
| Resveratrol lipid-carrier gellan/xanthan gel (Drug Deliv Transl Res 2018) | Rats, scopolamine, Morris water maze | 5x higher nasal permeation than suspension gel; better memory than oral suspension | Chemical model |
| Quercetin in situ gel (Pokharkar) | Rats, trimethyltin | Superior to donepezil on biochemistry and histology | Chemical model |
| Huperzine A pH-triggered gel (Acta Pharm Sin 2006) | Rats (CSF and blood PK), mice and rats (memory tests) | Compared nasal, IV and oral routes | Numbers not in excerpt |
| Tacrine Pluronic F-127 gel (Int J Pharm 2014, PMID 24709220) | Rats | Higher parent-drug brain uptake, lower metabolites vs oral | Healthy rats |
| Rivastigmine gels (earlier doc) | Mice | 0.54 %ID/g vs 0.16 for intranasal solution | Healthy mice |
| Timosaponin BII dual-sensitive nasal gel (Int J Pharm 2020;578:119115) | LPS-induced AD rats | Prevention of LPS-induced changes (cited in a review) | Details not seen |
| Collagen-loaded neprilysin, intranasal (Humpel, Front Aging Neurosci 2021) | Transgenic AD mice | Cleared amyloid plaques (cited in a review) | Collagen carrier; details not seen |

## 3. Injected depot and brain-environment gels

| Gel and cargo | Route and model | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hyaluronic acid gel with microstructured lipid carriers and albumin, donepezil (Pharmaceutics 2021;13:864) | Subcutaneous, rats | Release prolonged up to 7 days; Cmax 0.3x the control gel; biocompatible histology |
| PLGA-PEG-PLGA thermosensitive gel with curcumin (Antioxidants 2022;11:727) | Intramuscular, aluminium-chloride AD rats | Better Morris water maze performance, less amyloid-beta deposition, no toxicity reported |
| Methacrylated gelatin (GelMA) with galantamine (Curr Res Pharmacol Drug Discov 2022;3:100100) | Streptozotocin AD rats, 10 mg/kg | Less amyloid-beta aggregation; lower acetylcholinesterase and lipid peroxidation |
| Triglycerol monostearate-hyaluronan-NRG1 hydrogel, mimicking naked mole rat brain (Chem Eng J 2021) | Intracerebroventricular, AD mice incl. 16-month-old | Less amyloid-beta plaque and complement C1q in hippocampus; cognitive decline eased |
| Amyloid-capture peptide "AMYTRAP" gels (patent US 8,445,438; NIH SBIR grants) | Subcutaneous "detox depot", APPswe mice (planned) | In vitro capture of 10 ug amyloid-beta per 100 ul gel in 2 h (grant text). Safety in rats, rabbits and dogs reported "no toxicity" by the company. Mouse efficacy results not found |

## 4. Human

- No Alzheimer's hydrogel trial found. A 2022 commentary (Gong et al., J Transl Intern Med) and several reviews describe hydrogels as preclinical for Alzheimer's.
- A 2023 review of intranasal delivery in Alzheimer-type dementia (Taléns-Visconti, Pharmaceutics 15:1399) covers human intranasal work, but the excerpt does not show gel-based human studies.
- The human safety data for CNS-contact hydrogels in the earlier doc (DuraSeal dural sealant trial, 237 patients) apply here too.

## 5. What this adds for the project

- Alzheimer's has the deepest intranasal-gel animal literature of the four diseases, so it gives the largest comparison set for a gel-feature versus brain-uptake model (gelator, concentration, mucoadhesive, carrier, comparator, species).
- It repeats the same gaps: no human data, mostly chemical-induced models, mostly small-molecule cargo, repeated-dose safety of about 2 weeks.
- One transgenic-model nasal gel (D-penicillamine in APP/PS1 mice) and one intracerebroventricular gel (naked mole rat design) are the strongest animal evidence found.
