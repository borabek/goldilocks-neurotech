Scope

What we cut, and why

  • Calibrated sleep-time ranges. Built, then switched off. Observed coverage was 60% on BOAS and 20% externally against a 90% target, so the release gate disables the claim and exported intervals are null.
  • The label-free sleep-window rule. Tested, not adopted: it cut the external error from 235.9 to 177.5 minutes but made the headband worse, 31.8 to 45.3. Moved to next work.
  • The daytime experiment. Needs teammates recorded on the headset. Not run, so there is no pre-stimulus claim here. The live path itself was built and measured: scoring epoch by epoch as the night arrives costs 0.038 kappa against the same model scored retrospectively (0.728 → 0.690), with no lookahead.
  • A public repository and QR code. Team decision: no public repo tonight. The identifier is a content hash, not an invented link, and the folder is the artefact.
  • Drug, hydrogel and nanoparticle modules. Kept as a bounded research track outside the scored core. The coating/core model refuses to fit until matched pairs from ten studies exist — by its own rule, not by our choice.
  • Any clinical, ADHD or medication claim. Out of scope by construction: no paired outcomes, no children, no treatment data in anything we evaluated.
Protected throughout: the trained model, the frozen person-held-out evaluation, and the baselines. The CNN challenger was the one stretch item we did finish tonight — it beats the frozen model on the headband and then collapses on Sleep-EDF.

Falsifiability

What would change our mind

  • A matched baseline beating us. Two did. YASA beats us on Sleep-EDF, 0.675 against 0.579, and the CNN we trained tonight beats us on the headband, 0.728 against 0.661. We report both. The contribution was never "our model is best": it is the evaluation that shows which one to trust where.
  • Interval coverage near the nominal target. It was not: 60% and 20% against 90%. That is why the range claim is switched off rather than softened. Probability calibration improved (log loss 0.739 → 0.545); that is a different property.
  • A new population. Sleep-EDF was exactly that, and the summary claim failed on it. The honest boundary is the device and population we evaluated, not "wearables".
  • A better architecture than the frozen trunk. We built the obvious upgrade and it lost. Training the encoder jointly over 21-epoch sequences scored macro-F1 0.434 with grid sampling and 0.435 with random sampling, against 0.618 for the frozen trunk with a temporal head — a gap of 0.184, about 13× the 0.015 seed spread, and two independent sampling strategies agree.
  • In the research track, an audit that removes a win. That also happened: merging two duplicated study measurements moved the exposure model from beating its baseline to not beating it (159 title groups → 157 source groups).
More exposure, more epochs or more datasets without paired functional outcomes would still not establish benefit.

How it is built

Five pieces, each there because something failed without it

  • Per-recording robust scaling. Amplitude on a forehead headband depends on how well the electrode sits, and that changes between people and across a single night. Scaling each recording by its own median and MAD, rather than a global constant, is what lets one set of weights read a stranger's signal at all.
  • A small convolutional trunk. 44,581 parameters over the raw 30-second epoch. It is deliberately small: with 48 people to fit on, a larger trunk memorises individuals rather than learning what N2 looks like.
  • A temporal head on top. One epoch in isolation is genuinely ambiguous — N1 and wake share most of their spectrum. The head sees 21 epochs at once, which is roughly the span a human scorer uses when they look at the page before and after.
  • A sequence smoother. Sleep does not jump between stages at random; the transition matrix estimated from the fitting nights encodes that, and a forward-backward pass over the night removes single-epoch flicker that no scorer would produce.
  • Temperature calibration. The raw network is overconfident. One scalar, fitted on six people who are in neither the fitting nor the test set, takes log loss from 0.739 to 0.545 and expected calibration error from 0.128 to 0.087. It changes no prediction, only how much the prediction claims to know.
The live path keeps all five but gives up the lookahead: a trailing window replaces whole-recording scaling and the smoother runs forward only.

The architecture question we actually tested

Reusing a frozen trunk beat training the whole thing together

The obvious next step, once a trunk and a temporal head both work, is to stop treating them as separate stages and train one network end to end over whole sequences. We built it. It lost, and it lost by a margin that is not noise.
  • Grid sampling, stride 21: macro-F1 0.434, kappa 0.530.
  • Random window sampling, three exposures per epoch: macro-F1 0.435, kappa 0.481.
  • Frozen trunk with a temporal head: macro-F1 0.618, kappa 0.734, sleep-time error 8.7 minutes.
Two sampling strategies that share no code path landed on the two figures above, and both sat 0.184 below the staged model — about 13 times the 0.015 spread we measured across random seeds. The reading we take from it is unglamorous: with 48 people to fit on, a jointly trained encoder has enough freedom to fit the fitting set and not enough data to generalise from it. Freezing a trunk that was fitted on single epochs is a constraint, and the constraint is what helps.
We report it because the opposite is what we expected, and because a negative result that cost a night of compute is still evidence about where the ceiling sits. It also sharpens the main finding: the limit here is the number of people, not the number of epochs. More nights from the same device would not fix a model that has seen 48 of them — and the Sleep-EDF collapse says the same thing from the other direction.

How the code holds the line

The protocol is enforced by file boundaries, not by discipline

  • Fitting and evaluation live in different files. v2_evaluate.py cannot fit a model, a calibrator or a threshold — there is no code in it that could. If a number needs to change, it has to change somewhere visible.
  • One mask, written once. (y >= 0) & (y < 5) & good appears in every scorer. Comparing two models on two different masks is the easiest way to manufacture a win, so the mask is not a parameter anywhere.
  • The split is a file, not a convention. protocol.json names who is in the 48 fitting people, the six selection people, the six calibration people and the 20 test people. Selection reads the six; nothing reads the 20 until the end.
  • Every slide number is read at build time. This deck is generated by make_presentation.py from the saved result JSONs. There is no typed-in figure to go stale, which is why the deck can be rebuilt after a result changes and simply be right.
  • The seed spread is a gate, not a footnote. seed_variance.py puts a number (0.015 macro-F1) on how much two identical runs differ. Anything smaller than it is not reported as an improvement. Three claims were withdrawn under that rule.
  • The audit tests itself. leakage_audit.py ships eleven controlled cases with known answers, so a detector that silently stops working fails loudly instead.
One command re-runs the frozen evaluation offline in about half a minute, and it has now reproduced to the last digit on three machines and two operating systems.

Privacy and ethics

Public data, visible provenance, nothing covert

  • Every result uses public, de-identified releases — BOAS via OpenNeuro (CC0) and Sleep-EDF via PhysioNet (ODC-By 1.0), both credited with DOIs.
  • No teammate or attendee recording exists in this project. The optional day experiment that would have involved teammates was not run, so there is no consent question to answer after the fact.
  • No health inference about anyone at the event.
  • Local storage only. No upload, no public repository, no third-party service holds the data. The folder stays on the team's machine.
  • Provenance is part of the product: frozen protocol and model hashes, acquisition logs, published dataset checksums and per-person loader audits all ship with the results.
  • No clinical function. Nothing here diagnoses, titrates medication, or applies to children.
Consent to EEG collection would not have permitted a public face or video recording; that distinction is why the demo video shows only the viewer.
