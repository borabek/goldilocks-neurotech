BIOCONJUGATE · OXFORD NEUROTECH · STREAM 2

Getting a drug into the brain is hard.
Proving it got there is harder.

Our target is delivery for Alzheimer's, Parkinson's and ADHD. Before we claim anything about a therapy, we have to be able to trust a measurement. So we built the measurement discipline first.
THE CHALLENGE

In this field, claims outrun the measurements

Brain exposure in humans cannot be measured directly. A plasma level is not a brain level, and PET with a labelled drug is the best available option, not a convenient one.
ADHD has no validated imaging biomarker at all, which makes a symptom scale the endpoint — and a symptom scale cannot separate a delivery effect from a placebo one.
A method that cannot tell you when it is wrong is worth nothing here. So the first thing we built is not a therapy. It is an evaluation that fails loudly.
OUR APPROACH

Prove the discipline on data we can actually get

We have no patients and no imaging. What we do have is public neurophysiology, so we tested the method end to end on it: one model for wearable EEG sleep staging, frozen before testing, held out by person, then run unchanged on a second independent cohort.
Five stages every thirty seconds, each with a calibrated confidence. A held-out person from the public BOAS cohort. We recorded no one.
